Frequently Asked Questions (CADR, ACH & HEPA Buying Guide)

Evidence-based engineering answers to help you pick the right air purifier without overpaying.

What is CADR and how do I calculate the exact size I need?

CADR stands for Clean Air Delivery Rate, measured in Cubic Feet per Minute (CFM) or m³/h. To calculate your required CADR, multiply your room's Length × Width × Ceiling Height to get cubic volume, multiply by your target Air Changes Per Hour (ACH), and divide by 60. For example, a 300 sq ft room with 8 ft ceilings (2,400 cu ft) targeting 4 ACH requires a minimum CADR of (2,400 × 4) / 60 = 160 CFM.

How many Air Changes Per Hour (ACH) do I need for allergies vs. wildfire smoke?

For general dust maintenance, 2 ACH is sufficient. For seasonal allergies, asthma, and pet dander, AHAM and medical guidelines recommend at least 4 to 4.8 ACH (cleaning the room every 12.5 to 15 minutes). During active wildfire smoke events or heavy indoor odor spikes, size your purifier for 5 to 6 ACH.

Why do manufacturer room size ratings often seem exaggerated?

Many brands advertise maximum square footage based on just 1 or 2 ACH (one full air change per hour), which is far too slow for allergies or smoke. Always check the AHAM-verified 4.8 ACH room rating—or use our CADR calculator above to calculate true coverage based on your real ceiling height.

Is it better to buy an oversized air purifier and run it on low speed?

Yes. Buying a purifier with 30% to 50% higher CADR than your minimum requirement lets you run it on medium or low speed 24/7. You achieve the same 4+ ACH cleaning rate at whisper-quiet bedroom noise levels (22–32 dBA) while drawing far less electricity.

What is the difference between True HEPA (H13) and HEPA-type filters?

True HEPA filters are tested to capture at least 99.97% of airborne particles down to 0.3 microns (and medical-grade H13 captures 99.95% down to 0.1 microns). 'HEPA-type' or 'HEPA-style' filters lack strict certification and often let fine smoke particles and allergens pass right through.

Do HEPA air purifiers remove odors, wildfire gases, and VOCs?

HEPA fibers only trap solid particles (smoke soot, dust, pollen, mold spores, pet dander). To remove chemical VOCs, cooking smells, and pet odors, your purifier must also include an Activated Carbon stage—ideally pelletized carbon rather than a thin sprayed foam sheet.

How much electricity does running an air purifier 24/7 cost?

Modern brushless DC purifiers (such as Blueair, Levoit, and Coway) draw only 5–15 watts on medium speeds and 25–75 watts on maximum speed. Running a typical 30W medium setting 24/7 at $0.16/kWh costs roughly $42 per year—about $3.50 per month.

Should I run two medium purifiers or one giant unit in an open floor plan?

In L-shaped rooms, long open-concept living/kitchen areas, or homes with hallways, two medium high-CADR purifiers placed at opposite ends create much better air circulation and fewer dead zones than one massive unit sitting in a single corner.
